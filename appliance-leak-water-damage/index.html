Dealing With Appliance Leak Water Damage in Hawthorne

Nearly every appliance leak we handle comes from one of three sources. A washing machine supply hose can split and run at full pressure until someone shuts it off. With a dishwasher the leak is typically slow, coming from the door seal or the drain hose where the cabinets hide it. Behind a refrigerator, the thin tube feeding the ice maker may drip for months. Cleanup is different for each of them.

For homeowners in Hawthorne, we begin by sliding the machine out to see how far the water traveled. For a kitchen, the usual places are below the flooring and under the cabinets. Laundry room water goes into the wall at the back of the machine and the neighboring rooms, and with an upstairs laundry, the ceiling underneath. Standing water is extracted, absorbed moisture is measured, and drying equipment is sized to fit the wet area.

Do This First

What to Do Right Now

  1. Turn Off the ApplianceStop the cycle and unplug the machine if the outlet and floor around it are dry. If not, switch it off at the breaker instead.
  2. Close the Supply ValveBehind a washer, on the wall, are separate hot and cold valves. Look below the sink for the valve that feeds a dishwasher. A refrigerator line has a small valve below or behind the unit.
  3. Mop Up What You CanUse towels or a mop on whatever water is within reach, which keeps it from spreading beneath flooring and cabinets. Use a vacuum only if it is a wet and dry model.
  4. Keep the Door ClosedIf the washer or dishwasher has not drained, leave it shut. The entire load of water may spill across the floor if the door is opened mid-cycle.
  5. Photograph the Failed PartTake pictures of the hose, valve or fitting that failed and keep the part. It may be requested by the manufacturer of the appliance and by your insurance company.

Coverage

Does Insurance Cover It?

When a machine overflows unexpectedly or a hose bursts, that is typically treated as sudden and accidental, which many homeowners policies cover. What is typically paid is the water damage, while the failed part and the appliance are left out. A claim is harder when an ice maker line or dishwasher seeped slowly, since coverage usually does not extend to gradual leaks. Some insurers also ask about the age and upkeep of the hose. Hold on to the part that failed, photograph everything ahead of cleanup, and review what your policy says.

Common Causes

How It Usually Starts

  • Overloading the drum or adding excess detergent, either of which can make the machine overflow.
  • Ice maker lines, copper or plastic, that crack, kink or come loose where they connect.
  • Washer supply hoses made of rubber that get weak over the years and burst from the pressure.
  • Installation mistakes like a loose supply line connection or a dishwasher that does not sit level.
  • Worn dishwasher door gaskets, cracked drain hoses or a failed pump seal.
Why Speed Matters

What Happens if You Wait

Baseboards and Wall Bottoms

On a flooded kitchen or laundry floor, the baseboards and the lower edge of the drywall draw water upward. Often the worst of it is on the wall in back of the appliance, where it went unseen.

Mold Behind Appliances

The warm, dark space behind a refrigerator or dishwasher rarely gets checked. Long before it is discovered, a slow drip back there can be growing mold across the floor and wall.

Floors and What Is Under Them

A leak runs under laminate, hardwood or vinyl plank flooring with little resistance. The boards swell or cup, while the subfloor below remains wet well after the top looks dry.

Rooms Next Door

For hours, a failed washer hose can keep releasing water. It passes under walls into hallways, closets and bedrooms, and soaks carpet and pad well away from the laundry room.
