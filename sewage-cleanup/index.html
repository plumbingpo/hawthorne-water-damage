Of all the problems an owner can have, sewage coming up through the drains ranks among the most dangerous. Raw sewage contains dangerous bacteria, viruses, parasites and other pathogens, including E. coli, hepatitis, salmonella and giardia, that pose serious health risks to anyone who touches contaminated materials. Across Hawthorne, Hawthorne Restoration answers sewage emergencies 24/7, sending trained technicians who arrive with EPA-registered disinfectants and the right protective equipment so your property is decontaminated safely.

Sewage damage is classified as Category 3 (black water), the most contaminated and dangerous category of water damage. Because of it, cleanup follows the toughest rules in the trade, which means full protective gear, barriers to contain the area, tearing out any porous material that met sewage and disinfecting all that is left. Our technicians follow IICRC S500 standards for Category 3 water damage so your property is restored to safe occupancy standards.

We clear the standing sewage first, then pull contaminated water out of structural materials, remove carpet, padding, insulation, drywall and any other porous item that was in contact with sewage for proper disposal, disinfect each hard surface, treat with antimicrobial products and finish with structural drying, which prevents secondary mold. We also deodorize the affected area with industrial-grade hydroxyl generators and thermal fogging equipment to eliminate lingering sewage odors.

Signs It Is Time for Sewage Cleanup

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you time, money, and prevent significant damage to your property. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • Unexplained illness or gastrointestinal symptoms among household members
  • Dark, dirty water or sewage rising out of a tub, toilet or floor drain
  • A cleanout that weeps sewage, or wet foul patches in the lawn along the sewer path
  • Wet spots on floors or walls accompanied by foul odors
  • Toilets or drains that gurgle whenever you run water somewhere else
  • A toilet overflow containing waste that has spread onto flooring or carpet
  • Strong sewage or rotten egg odors coming from drains or lower levels

Sewage Cleanup Prevention Tips

Taking preventive measures can help you avoid costly water damage issues down the road. Follow these tips from our IICRC-certified technicians:

  • Keep cooking oil, grease and food scraps out of the drain, where they build into clogs
  • Roots from trees close to the sewer line work their way into pipes and block them, so have them cleared
  • Install a backwater valve on your sewer connection to prevent backups from the municipal system
  • Keep downspouts and sump pump discharge out of the sanitary sewer so it does not overload in heavy rain
  • Schedule a camera inspection of the sewer line every 2 to 3 years so trouble is found early
  • Know the location and condition of your sewer cleanout for emergency access

Sewage Cleanup: What Homeowners Ask

We typically recommend temporary relocation during sewage cleanup, especially if the contamination in your home is extensive. There are health risks from pathogens that become airborne and from the products we use to decontaminate. Our recommendation will depend on what we find in your particular home.
Removing the sewage and decontaminating the property is usually done in 1 to 3 days. Complete restoration, including material replacement, may take 1 to 3 weeks depending on the extent of damage. Throughout, the focus is on returning your home to a safe, livable state without delay.
Nobody, pets included, should walk through the area the sewage reached. Do not flush, run faucets or start the washer or dishwasher, because added water feeds the backup. If the panel and thermostat can be reached without contact with sewage, shut off electricity to that area and stop the air system. Skip the mop and bucket, scrub any exposed skin and wait for a trained crew.
Most backups start with a blockage in the home's sewer line. Roots from nearby trees, flushed wipes and accumulated grease account for a large share of those clogs. Older lines can also crack or collapse, and heavy rain can overload the municipal sewer and push sewage back through floor drains. A camera inspection after cleanup shows which one you are dealing with.
Some things can be kept and others cannot. Hard, non-porous items such as metal, glass, sealed tile and solid plastic can usually be cleaned and disinfected. Anything porous the sewage touched must go, which covers mattresses, upholstered furniture, drywall, insulation, carpet and padding. For insurance purposes, our crew keeps a list of everything that had to be removed.

Why Professional Sewage Cleanup Matters

This is one cleanup you should never take on yourself. The biological hazards in raw sewage require specialized training, proper personal protective equipment (Tyvek suits, respirators, protective gloves and boots) and EPA-registered disinfection products that are not available to consumers. Exposure to sewage pathogens can cause serious illness, including gastrointestinal disease, hepatitis, respiratory infections and skin infections.

Professional sewage cleanup also requires proper disposal of contaminated materials as biohazard waste. Putting carpet, insulation or drywall soaked with sewage out with household trash breaks the law and endangers sanitation workers and your community. Licensed biohazard disposal facilities take this waste from certified restoration firms, which keep the required protocols in place.
